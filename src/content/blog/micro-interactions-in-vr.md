In flat interfaces, micro-interactions can be decorative.

In VR, they are orientation tools. Users rely on subtle response cues to confirm that objects are interactive and that gestures have been recognized. Timing, easing, and spatial consistency become part of the cognitive safety net.

The rule of thumb is simple: make feedback immediate, predictable, and physically plausible.
